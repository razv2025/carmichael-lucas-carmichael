An odd squarefree composite integer is a Williams number if it is both Carmichael and Lucas-Carmichael; none is known. For a four-prime Williams number $n=p_1p_2p_3p_4$ we restate, with a self-contained proof, the bounds of McIntosh and Dipra, and derive from them an enumeration that fixes $(p_1,p_2)$, places $p_3p_4$ in a single residue class modulo $\mathrm{lcm}((p_1^2-1)/2,(p_2^2-1)/2)$, and rejects almost every candidate with one multiplication; both devices are adapted from the tabulation of Carmichael numbers. Certified computations prove that no such number satisfies $n\le10^{32}$ or $p_1\le10^{6}$, extending McIntosh and Dipra's $n>10^{24}$, $p_1>2\times10^4$. Separately, we classify every prime factor $p$ of each of the $308,279,939$ Carmichael numbers $n<10^{24}$ according to whether $p+1$ divides $n+1$, $n-1$ or neither: no Williams number occurs, at most four primes of any $n$ satisfy $(p+1)\mid(n+1)$, and the resulting lists reproduce three OEIS sequences. For Williams numbers with any number of prime factors we point out that Puddefoot's recent census of the Chebyshev-Carmichael numbers below $10^{30}$, a class containing them, implies that none is smaller than $10^{30}$. Code, logs and certification records are archived.
