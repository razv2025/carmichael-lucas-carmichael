An odd squarefree composite integer is a Williams number if it is both Carmichael and Lucas--Carmichael; none is known. For a four-prime Williams number $n=p_1p_2p_3p_4$ we give a self-contained restatement, with a slight sharpening, of the bounds due to McIntosh and Dipra, and derive from them an enumeration that fixes $(p_1,p_2)$, places $p_3p_4$ in a single residue class modulo $lcm((p_1^2-1)/2,(p_2^2-1)/2)$, and rejects almost every candidate with one multiplication. Certified computations prove that no such number satisfies $n\le10^{30}$ or $p_1\le500,000$, extending McIntosh and Dipra's $n>10^{24}$, $p_1>2\times10^4$. Separately, a factor-and-test scan of all $308,279,939$ Carmichael numbers below $10^{24}$ finds no Williams number. Code, logs and certification records are archived.
