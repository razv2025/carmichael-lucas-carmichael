An odd squarefree composite integer is a Williams number if it is both Carmichael and Lucas-Carmichael; none is known. For a four-prime Williams number $n=p_1p_2p_3p_4$ we restate, with a self-contained proof, the bounds of McIntosh and Dipra, and derive from them an enumeration that fixes $(p_1,p_2)$, places $p_3p_4$ in a single residue class modulo $\mathrm{lcm}((p_1^2-1)/2,(p_2^2-1)/2)$, and rejects almost every candidate with one multiplication; both devices are adapted from the tabulation of Carmichael numbers. Certified computations prove that if $n$ is a Williams number with exactly four prime factors and $p_1$ is its smallest prime factor, then $n>10^{32}$ and $p_1>10^{6}$; the second bound holds without any upper bound on $n$. McIntosh and Dipra had $n>10^{24}$ and $p_1>2\times10^4$. Separately, using the Shallue-Webster tabulation of the $308,279,939$ Carmichael numbers below $10^{24}$ as input, we independently test every entry for the Williams conditions and classify each prime factor $p$ of each $n$ according to whether $p+1$ divides $n+1$, $n-1$ or neither: no Williams number occurs, at most four primes of any $n$ satisfy $(p+1)\mid(n+1)$, and the resulting lists agree with three OEIS sequences over the ranges compared and extend two of them. For Williams numbers with any number of prime factors we point out that Puddefoot's recent census of the Chebyshev-Carmichael numbers below $10^{30}$, a class containing them, implies, assuming that census is complete, that none is smaller than $10^{30}$. Finally we prove that no product of two or more distinct linear forms with positive slopes can satisfy the Williams divisibilities identically, which excludes Chernick-type constructions (not individual Williams numbers). Code, logs and certification records are archived.
